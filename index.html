Potential IFC additionality — hypotheses to test

Longer tenor

Compare debt service and coverage with a longer-tenor facility.

Mobilisation

Test how additional lender participation changes the funding gap and financing mix.

Risk mitigation

Identify whether guarantees or risk-sharing could address specific risks; no benefit is assumed in the model.

IFC participation is hypothetical. No IFC terms, appetite, eligibility or additionality conclusion is assumed.

Draft model methodology: annual periods; evenly phased construction CAPEX and proportional debt drawdowns; capitalised IDC based on average annual drawn balance; straight-line depreciation over the operating period; interest tax deductibility with loss carry-forward; level-payment amortisation after opening; project NPV based on unlevered after-tax cash flow; equity IRR based on construction contributions and operating distributions. This is a screening prototype, not a lender-grade model or investment recommendation.

Evidence classification

Verified primary

Claim directly supported by a regulator, ministry, contract or issuer disclosure.

Reported third-party

Claim reported by a reputable external source but not yet verified against primary documentation.

Analytical inference

THEMIS interpretation, such as potential investor fit or an IFC engagement angle.

Assumption / unknown

Scenario input or information gap. Never silently converted into a fact.

Diagnostic synthesis

Fiscal vulnerability

Debt and interest burden. High debt and a large overall deficit can crowd out public investment and constrain state-level PPP payment capacity. Assess each grantor and guarantee separately.

External resilience

Buffers and FX exposure coexist. Reserves and a flexible exchange rate absorb shocks, but currency volatility still matters for foreign-currency debt and imported equipment.

Structural constraint

Productivity and connectivity. Human-capital gaps, uneven logistics and regulatory complexity weigh on productivity and project execution.

IFC Country Private Sector Diagnostics

Methodology for diagnosing private-sector constraints.

IFC CPSD ↗

Main barriers to private investment

  1. Cost and tenor of finance: high domestic rates and limited long-tenor local-currency funding can affect project affordability.
  2. Fiscal / subnational capacity: PPP payment commitments and public counterpart obligations require fiscal-risk assessment.
  3. Regulatory complexity: tax, licensing and multi-level responsibilities raise transaction costs.
  4. Infrastructure gaps: regional disparities and network bottlenecks affect demand and execution.
  5. Project preparation: land, environmental licensing and contract design can delay financial close.

AI-enabled business development · Proposed capabilities

AI Agents & Workflows

Six specialised agents could support the investment and business-development lifecycle — from understanding a market to preparing an informed client conversation. They are proposed capabilities, not live autonomous agents in this static demo.

One connected intelligence workflow

From market signals to actionable engagement

The agents could share a common evidence base, pass structured findings between workflows and prepare outputs for review by an investment officer. Deterministic calculations and human judgement would remain separate from language-model interpretation.

Six specialised agents

Each agent could answer a distinct business-development question and produce a reviewable output.

01 · Country Intelligence

Business question: Where could market conditions support private investment?

Could analyse: macroeconomic and fiscal conditions, external risks, financial depth, regulation, infrastructure gaps and sector dynamics.

Could produce: country and sector briefs, investment barriers, risk signals and questions for further diligence.

02 · Opportunity Sourcing

Business question: Which projects could become relevant investment opportunities?

Could analyse: infrastructure plans, PPP and concession pipelines, procurement notices, sponsors, project maturity and changes in official documents.

Could produce: a source-linked opportunity pipeline, project fact sheets, maturity assessments and origination hypotheses.

03 · Investor Intelligence

Business question: Which investors or companies could be relevant partners?

Could analyse: mandates, ownership, strategy, portfolios, geographic exposure, track record, instruments and disclosed investment constraints.

Could produce: investor profiles, comparable transactions, capability maps and evidence gaps — without inferring appetite from incomplete information.

04 · Project Screening

Business question: Which opportunities could merit deeper assessment?

Could analyse: project structure, revenue model, demand evidence, CAPEX/OPEX, implementation, regulatory, integrity and environmental and social risks.

Could produce: a preliminary screening, risk register, diligence checklist and financing questions. Financial metrics would be calculated by deterministic tools using verified inputs.

05 · Research & Due Diligence

Business question: What evidence supports the analysis, and what is still missing?

Could analyse: tender documents, concession agreements, annual reports, regulator decisions and financial disclosures; extract claims, tables and document versions.

Could produce: source-linked evidence briefs, extracted data, discrepancy flags and a clear record of unresolved questions.

06 · Business Development

Business question: Who should be approached, about what, and what should happen next?

Could combine: investor and project intelligence, relationship history, potential matching rationale, country context and evidence gaps.

Could produce: prioritised engagement ideas, client briefs, meeting preparation, stakeholder maps and draft follow-ups for human review.

How the agents could work together

Illustrative workflow · Brazil infrastructure example

1 · Identify the market context

Country Intelligence could flag a sector opportunity and the main macroeconomic, regulatory and market constraints.

2 · Find potential projects

Opportunity Sourcing could identify relevant concessions or infrastructure projects and create a source-linked longlist.

3 · Verify the evidence

Research & Due Diligence could retrieve the underlying notices, contracts and disclosures, extract key information and flag gaps.

4 · Assess the opportunity and capital

Project Screening could organise the initial risk and bankability questions; Investor Intelligence could identify investors whose disclosed capabilities may be relevant.

5 · Prepare engagement

Business Development could bring the findings together into a prioritised client brief, potential discussion points and proposed next steps.

What the agents could do

  • Search, extract, classify and synthesise information.
  • Connect investors, projects and country context.
  • Identify evidence gaps, inconsistencies and diligence questions.
  • Prepare source-linked briefs and draft materials for review.

What would remain under human control

  • Investment, credit, integrity and E&S decisions.
  • Validation of extracted facts and source quality.
  • Approval of investor outreach and external communications.
  • Professional judgement on IFC role, additionality and transaction suitability.

What is implemented in this demo

The current static demo includes sourced example datasets, live World Bank indicator retrieval and deterministic tools for selected scoring, screening, financial calculations and briefing templates. The six agents described above are a proposed operating model; live LLM orchestration, document ingestion, persistent memory and automated monitoring are not connected.
