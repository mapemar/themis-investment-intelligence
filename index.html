THEMIS / Investment Intelligence PlatformDEMO · WORKSPACE
Investment intelligence · Opportunity origination · Business development

Find the opportunity.
Connect the capital.

THEMIS is an investment intelligence and business development concept for identifying infrastructure opportunities in emerging markets, understanding potential capital providers, testing investor–project fit and preparing evidence-led engagement.

One connected workflow: understand the market → originate opportunities → identify capital → assess fit → test the transaction → engage investors → retain the evidence.
Platform purpose

From country context to an investable conversation

THEMIS brings country intelligence, project pipeline research, investor intelligence and preliminary transaction analysis into one traceable workflow. Its central question is: which opportunity fits which investor, what could IFC contribute, and what should the business development team do next?

The platform supports screening and preparation. It does not make investment decisions or replace commercial, technical, legal, environmental and social due diligence.

Interpretation discipline

A potential IFC relevance assessment is a hypothesis for business development. It does not establish investor appetite, financing availability, IFC eligibility, additionality or approval.

Data governance & source policy

Preferred hierarchy: audited annual reports and regulatory filings; official investment policies and strategy documents; portfolio and transaction disclosures; official press releases; institutional datasets; licensed commercial providers. Every material data point should retain source URL, publication date, observation date, retrieval date, metric definition and verification status.

Commercial data providers (for example Preqin, PitchBook, LSEG, Bloomberg and Orbis) are potential licensed enrichment sources, not connected data feeds in this static demo. Re-use and storage depend on licence terms.

Potential IFC additionality — hypotheses to test

Longer tenor

Compare debt service and coverage with a longer-tenor facility.

Mobilisation

Test how additional lender participation changes the funding gap and financing mix.

Risk mitigation

Identify whether guarantees or risk-sharing could address specific risks; no benefit is assumed in the model.

IFC participation is hypothetical. No IFC terms, appetite, eligibility or additionality conclusion is assumed.

Draft model methodology: annual periods; evenly phased construction CAPEX and proportional debt drawdowns; capitalised IDC based on average annual drawn balance; straight-line depreciation over the operating period; interest tax deductibility with loss carry-forward; level-payment amortisation after opening; project NPV based on unlevered after-tax cash flow; equity IRR based on construction contributions and operating distributions. This is a screening prototype, not a lender-grade model or investment recommendation.

Diagnostic synthesis

Fiscal vulnerability

Debt and interest burden. High debt and a large overall deficit can crowd out public investment and constrain state-level PPP payment capacity. Assess each grantor and guarantee separately.

External resilience

Buffers and FX exposure coexist. Reserves and a flexible exchange rate absorb shocks, but currency volatility still matters for foreign-currency debt and imported equipment.

Structural constraint

Productivity and connectivity. Human-capital gaps, uneven logistics and regulatory complexity weigh on productivity and project execution.

IFC Country Private Sector Diagnostics

Methodology for diagnosing private-sector constraints.

Main barriers to private investment

  1. Cost and tenor of finance: high domestic rates and limited long-tenor local-currency funding can affect project affordability.
  2. Fiscal / subnational capacity: PPP payment commitments and public counterpart obligations require fiscal-risk assessment.
  3. Regulatory complexity: tax, licensing and multi-level responsibilities raise transaction costs.
  4. Infrastructure gaps: regional disparities and network bottlenecks affect demand and execution.
  5. Project preparation: land, environmental licensing and contract design can delay financial close.

Output: preliminary project assessment, risk register, diligence checklist and investment questions.

05 · Pipeline Monitoring Agent

Analyses: changes to tender status, auction dates, project documents, sponsors, bidders and source freshness.

Output: change alerts, updated pipeline records, source diffs and recommended follow-up actions.

06 · Investor Engagement Agent

Analyses: investor relationship history, prior interactions, relevant opportunities, meeting notes and pending commitments.

Output: account brief, meeting agenda, stakeholder map, action log and follow-up draft. It never invents contacts or meetings.

07 · Project Finance Agent

Analyses: cash-flow inputs, sources and uses, debt sizing, repayment profile, DSCR/LLCR and scenario sensitivities using deterministic calculations.

Output: financing case, covenant table, sensitivity results and explicit model limitations — only where input data exist.

08 · Evidence & Document Agent

Analyses: contracts, tender notices, annual reports, regulatory decisions and financial disclosures; extracts claims, tables and document versions.

Output: searchable evidence register, source-linked claim cards, extracted data and unresolved contradictions.

09 · Country Intelligence Agent

Analyses: macroeconomic conditions, FX and convertibility, sovereign/sub-sovereign context, regulatory institutions, PPP frameworks and development finance activity.

Output: country risk brief, macro dashboard, sector context and country-level diligence questions.

10 · Orchestrator / Investment Brief Agent

Analyses: the task, required specialist workflows, cross-agent findings, source quality, contradictions and missing inputs.

Output: prioritised workplan and integrated origination note: investor, project, fit, IFC role, risks, evidence gaps and next actions.

How agents work together

Example: the Opportunity Origination Agent discovers a Brazilian toll-road concession; the Evidence Agent retrieves its notice and contract; the Country Agent adds Brazil context; the Project Assessment and Finance Agents examine risks and financing inputs; the Investor Intelligence Agent profiles CDP; the Matching Agent compares capabilities and project needs; the Engagement Agent prepares a discussion plan; the Orchestrator consolidates the investment brief.

Conceptual agent design These are proposed specialised workflows, not ten live autonomous LLM agents in the current static website. The demo contains selected interactive workflows and deterministic calculations; live model inference, scheduled monitoring and persistent agent memory require a backend.

What AI does

  • Extracts and summarises relevant information.
  • Classifies investors, sectors, geographies and projects.
  • Highlights inconsistencies, missing information and diligence questions.
  • Drafts investor briefs and meeting notes.

What AI must not do

  • Invent traffic, CAPEX, IRR, DSCR or debt terms.
  • Make credit, integrity, E&S or investment decisions.
  • Present assumptions as facts or infer investor appetite.
  • Replace due diligence or professional judgement.
